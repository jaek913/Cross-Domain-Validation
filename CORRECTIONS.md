## C-2026-09-30 | Onset detector fires at the season boundary in 13 of 27 seasons (§5.1.2, C-03, P1) | Author | Disclosure + secondary readout; no ledger value changed. Amended same day: first version said 10 of 27 (see Reproduction)

**Nature of the error.** The registered onset rule — first week in MMWR 40–20 at which SMA4 − SMA12 of national weighted ILI exceeds 0.2 pp — does not require the divergence to *cross* 0.2 inside the season window. Recomputed season by season on CDC ILINet (Delphi Epidata mirror, pulled 2026-09-30, regridded to the paper's continuous MMWR grid with the pre-2003 summer weeks zero-filled exactly as in the pinned file), the detector fires on the **first eligible week (MMWR 40) in 13 of the 27 paired seasons**: 1998-99, 2001-02, 2002-03, 2011-12, 2013-14, 2014-15, 2016-17, 2017-18, 2018-19, 2019-20, 2022-23, 2023-24, 2025-26. In eight of those the divergence was already above 0.2 at week 40 and never fell below it before the level comparator fired, so the recorded onset week is the calendar boundary, not a signal event. The pattern is recent: 9 of the last 12 paired seasons. The three early cases (1998-99, 2001-02, 2002-03) are seasons in which ILINet did not report weeks 21–39; the pinned file carries those weeks as 0.0, so SMA12 at week 40 is depressed by the fill and the divergence there rests partly on the data convention rather than the series. In the 21 paired seasons with year-round reporting (2003-04 onward) the count is 10 of 21. The level comparator (baseline = mean of the season's first four weeks, +1.0 pp) cannot fire before week 44 by construction, so a boundary firing guarantees the divergence a head start of at least four weeks regardless of what the series does.

**Effect on the reported figures.** Registered rule, reproduced on the 2026-09-30 pull: mean divergence lead over peak 14.148 wk, mean level lead 5.926 wk, advantage 8.222 wk, divergence first in 26 of 27 — all exact to the paper. Boundary seasons vs the rest: advantage 10.0 wk in the 13 boundary seasons, 6.6 wk in the other 14. Under a **strict crossing rule** (divergence must move from ≤ 0.2 to > 0.2 inside the window): scorable in 18 of 27 seasons; mean divergence lead 13.72 wk vs level 5.78 wk; advantage 7.94 wk; divergence first in 18 of 18. Year-round-reporting era only (2003-04 onward, 21 seasons): registered advantage 8.19 wk, 21 of 21; crossing rule scorable in 13, advantage 6.85 wk, 13 of 13. Season 2025-26: registered rule wk40 vs level wk48 (8-wk lead, as shown on the live board); strict crossing rule wk47 vs wk48 (1 wk). Season 1997-98: the divergence is undefined until week 51 (series start), so its recorded onset (wk51) and the level comparator's earlier firing (wk47) are a data-start artifact; it is the single "level-first" season in the 26-of-27 count.

**Change made.** The mechanism claim stands (divergence still first in every scorable season under the strict rule) but the headline "8.222 weeks" (abstract, §5.1.2, C-03) is inflated by boundary firings — by about 0.3 wk over all 27 seasons and by about 1.3 wk in the year-round-reporting era — and must be read alongside the crossing-rule figures of 7.94 wk (all seasons) and 6.85 wk (2003-04 onward). No `claims.lock` entry is altered; this is a disclosure. `verify.py` unaffected. **Registration addendum, dated before MMWR week 40 data exists:** for 2026-27 a secondary *crossing onset* readout (first in-window week at which D moves from ≤ 0.2 to > 0.2) is added to the live board and to `PREDICTIONS_TRACKER.md`, reported beside P1. P1 is scored exactly as registered; if D is above 0.2 at week 40 and never dips, the crossing readout is recorded as "not scorable" and any P1 pass is flagged *boundary-assisted*. The registered rule is not changed mid-test.

**Reproduction.** `verification/flu_onset_boundary_diagnostic.py` (MD5 e088d985ba3a56ea8595683b8b095d27) regenerates every figure above; per-season table `verification/flu_onset_boundary_diagnostic.csv` (MD5 58ca65e4e7482a8b52a411ab00dfe1d7); pinned pull `verification/ilinet_nat_pull_2026-09-30.json` (MD5 67e5ee1ea4fb57ab550c0ac9c7f0d16e). The live board (`prediction-flu.html`) computes the same block from the paper's own `e4_flu_onset` and `operators` modules and agrees. **Same-day amendment.** The first version of this entry (commit 4aba730) reported 10 of 27 and a 7.17-wk crossing advantage from a diagnostic (MD5 cbfda6f8557cd2ce4ddf8260fdea1533) that ran on the raw API rows with the pre-2003 summer gap absent rather than zero-filled, so its week-40 averages reached back into the previous spring; that version also mis-attributed its 13.63-wk divergence lead to data vintage. The year-round-era list of 10 seasons is identical under both conventions. The superseded figures are recorded here so the change is visible.

**Who identified it.** The author, on noticing that the live divergence (0.312 at 2026w36) was above the trigger before the window opened and that the prior season had fired on its first eligible week.
